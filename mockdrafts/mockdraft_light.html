Beyond the single model

Diorama & environment studies

Squads and heroes are most of the bench time, but the same process scales up to a full scene — basing, backdrop, and lighting built to match the miniatures rather than added as an afterthought.

Photo placeholder
Environment study · Astra Militarum vs. daemon incursion

Built like a squad, staged like a scene

The rock ledge is carved and layered the same way weathering gets built up on armor: several thin passes rather than one heavy one, so the transitions between materials read as natural instead of applied.

The cloud backdrop is painted in translucent layers behind the display glass, lit to throw real object-source light onto the models in front of it, so the OSL on the miniatures and the backdrop's glow are doing the same job from two directions.

Method

Same five steps, every time

Nothing about the process is secret. It's the same order whether it's one hero model or a forty-model horde — just repeated more times.

  1. 01

    Prime

    Zenithal, black to grey, so the value map already exists before any color goes on.

  2. 02

    Base coat

    Flat, even coverage in the true color. No highlights yet — just committing to the palette.

  3. 03

    Layer & blend

    Wet-blend or thin layered highlights built up from the base, worked while the edges are still workable.

  4. 04

    Wash & weather

    Recess shade first, then chipping, pigment, and rust — only where wear would actually happen.

  5. 05

    Seal & varnish

    Matte or satin coat locks the blend in and makes the model safe to actually put on a table.

Slow paint. Sharp edges. No shortcuts.

About

Dave

Dave has been painting miniatures for about nine years now, mostly late at night, mostly one squad at a time. Two armies get most of the bench time: Astra Militarum, painted patient and weathered, and Khorne, painted loud on purpose.

The kit hasn't changed much in years: a synthetic size 1 and size 0 for most of a model, an embarrassing number of decade-old drybrushes reserved just for weathering, and a wet palette that's outlived three different desks. Everything gets zenithal-primed black to grey and finished with a matte varnish — that part isn't optional.

Before any of that, he spent several years as an Army officer — which is less a costume detail and more a habit that stuck: plan the session, work the order of operations, don't call it finished until it actually is.

When he's not at the painting desk he's usually on a rock face somewhere, which explains the steady hands.

"If a technique makes six models look one degree more like they belong together, it's worth the extra hour. If it just makes one model look fancier sitting alone on a shelf, that's probably ego, not craft."

What to include in that first email

None of this is a form to fill out, just a shortlist so a first reply can come back with a real scope and quote instead of a round of back-and-forth questions.

  • 01

    Army / faction

    Astra Militarum, Khorne, or otherwise, plus any specific detachment or warband theme in mind.

  • 02

    Model count

    How many models, and which kit or box if it's known, so scope and timeline can be estimated accurately.

  • 03

    Reference images

    Box art, WIP shots, or a Pinterest board. Links are fine. Helps pin down palette and finish level up front.

  • 04

    Timeline

    Any hard deadline (an event, a birthday), or flexible if there isn't one.
